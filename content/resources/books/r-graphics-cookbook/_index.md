Winston Chang's practical guide collects more than 150 recipes for making high-quality graphs in R without having to master every detail of R's graphics systems first.
Each recipe states a concrete problem, gives a solution you can adapt to your own data, and then explains how and why the solution works.

This second edition is built largely around the updated `ggplot2` package and adds more material on the visual design of graphics.
It is aimed at scientists, engineers, programmers, and data analysts who already know some R.
You can look up a specific task when you need it, or simply browse for ideas about what kinds of plots are possible.
An appendix explains the ideas behind `ggplot2`, including its terminology, stats, and themes.

The recipes cover a wide range of tasks, including how to:

- Explore data quickly with scatter plots, line graphs, bar graphs, histograms, and box plots
- Build and customize bar graphs, line graphs, and scatter plots
- Summarize distributions with histograms, density curves, violin plots, and dot plots
- Add annotations, error bars, and mathematical expressions to help readers interpret a plot
- Control axes, legends, facets, themes, and overall appearance
- Choose color palettes, including colorblind-friendly options
- Create network graphs, heat maps, maps, and 3D scatter plots
- Export graphics to PDF, SVG, and bitmap formats, and reshape data with tidyverse tools

The second edition is free to read online at the book's website.
