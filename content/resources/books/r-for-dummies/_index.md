Andrie de Vries and Joris Meys wrote this friendly guide for anyone who wants to learn R, whether you have never programmed before or are a seasoned statistician or data analyst who finds R hard to pick up.
It also doubles as a handy technical reference for experienced R users.
In the familiar For Dummies style, it explains concepts in plain language and backs them up with practical examples, step-by-step exercises, and sample code.

The book starts with installing and configuring R and RStudio and getting comfortable with the fundamentals of the language.
From there it moves through R's core data structures, writing your own functions, and controlling program flow, then shifts to the everyday work of getting data in and out of R, reshaping it, and summarizing it.
Later chapters cover statistical tests and models and three different approaches to graphics.
A closing Part of Tens shows how to do common Microsoft Excel tasks in R and shares tips on finding and using packages.

Topics include:

- Working with vectors, matrices, arrays, data frames, and lists
- Handling text, factors, dates, and times
- Writing functions, controlling program flow, and debugging your code
- Importing and exporting data, including data from Excel
- Subsetting, merging, sorting, and reshaping data sets
- Summarizing data, testing differences, and fitting regression and ANOVA models
- Creating plots with base graphics, `lattice`, and `ggplot2`

A companion R package, `rfordummies`, accompanies the book.
