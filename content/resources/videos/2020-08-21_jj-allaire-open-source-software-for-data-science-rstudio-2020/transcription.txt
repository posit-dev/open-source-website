<h3>Why free and open source software matters for science</h3>

<p><a data-t="554">So I want to now say, you know, why is free and open source software for science and data science so important? <blockquote><a data-t="659">We sometimes with free software focus too much on the fact that the software has no cost. But the most important thing is that it comes without restrictions.</a></blockquote>

<p><a data-t="615">And they actually the new summarizes kind of the four essential freedoms of free and</a> <a data-t="621">open source software. <p><a data-t="646">So what are some of the reasons why we want to prefer free and open source software? <p><a data-t="874">And finally, what I talked about at the beginning, accessibility. <h3>RStudio's model: open source sustained by commercial products</h3>

<p><a data-t="1257">So, if you look a little bit at the history of the company,</a> <a data-t="1264">2008, late 2008, I believe, when we started, it was, as I said before, just one or two</a> <a data-t="1270">or three people working on open source software. So we did that, and you can see over the last seven</a> <a data-t="1302">years or so, we've produced a huge amount of open source software. We have, I think,</a> <a data-t="1306">over 250 open source projects that we are active developers on. <p><a data-t="1330">So I think we've figured out a model by which we can still produce open source software, but do it in this kind</a> <a data-t="1339">of sustainable, well-funded way. <h3>Corporations as stewards: the problem of shareholder primacy</h3>

<p><a data-t="1486">So we're pleased with this, but it begs the question, again, I said about</a> <a data-t="1494">corporations, and are we trustworthy? <p><a data-t="1550">So I'm going to go</a> <a data-t="1556">back, this is a little bit elemental, but I think it's not something that I had thought</a> <a data-t="1561">really carefully and critically about until a few years ago. What actually is a corporation?</a> <a data-t="1566">Why do they even exist? And what a corporation is, is actually, it's a virtual person. So</a> <a data-t="1574">it's acting as a person, legally a person, but it's actually, it's constituted by a group</a> <a data-t="1580">of people. So why do we even have corporations? Well, what did we do before corporations?</a> <a data-t="1587">Before corporations, any business that existed was either undertaken by an individual or</a> <a data-t="1593">maybe a partnership of individuals. And so the individuals were actually personally liable</a> <a data-t="1598">for everything the enterprise did. <p><a data-t="1620">So as the industrial age progressed, this</a> <a data-t="1628">model sort of didn't seem adequate, and the first corporations were actually formed by</a> <a data-t="1633">Royal Act, the East India Company being one of the most significant examples of that.</a> <a data-t="1638">And then as governments realized that we needed things like bridges, railways, banks, utilities,</a> <a data-t="1645">we need to operate at a different scale than individuals have been able to operate at.</a> <a data-t="1649">So they created this instrument called a corporation that they believed needed this sort of absolving</a> <a data-t="1656">of liability and additional capital that the corporate model provided for.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="1661">So if you think about then the essential nature of a corporation, it's an institution that</a> <a data-t="1666">is actually created by government in order to benefit the societies that govern. But if you look at that second bullet, you know, about without</a> <a data-t="1684">fear of personal liability, that actually is a recipe for bad behavior, and we've seen</a> <a data-t="1688">that play out as well. <p><a data-t="1697">So if you think about the legal theories of what the primary purpose of a corporation</a> <a data-t="1702">is, there's actually two competing theories. One is stakeholder. <p><a data-t="1730">There's another theory, which is shareholder primacy, and it's a pretty raw notion, which</a> <a data-t="1737">is the purpose of a corporation is to maximize value for shareholders within the bounds of</a> <a data-t="1742">law. <p><a data-t="1757">And there's been a couple significant cases that establish that, or many cases that have</a> <a data-t="1763">sort of built that up. <p><a data-t="1788">Another case, a little more contemporary,</a> <a data-t="1792">Revlon was faced with an acquisition proposal that actually the shareholders, it appealed</a> <a data-t="1797">to the shareholders, it was a good deal for them, but the board felt that it was going</a> <a data-t="1801">to not be a good deal for the employees or the bondholders, people who actually held</a> <a data-t="1805">debt. <p><a data-t="1820">And many people find this lacking. <p><a data-t="1865">So in response to this, a bunch of states have actually adopted legislation that permit,</a> <a data-t="1871">note permit not require, permit directors to consider things other than shareholder</a> <a data-t="1876">value. <h3>The benefit corporation model</h3>

<p><a data-t="1904">So what can be done to change the system? So</a> <a data-t="1910">I don't know how many of you have heard of the company AND1. Have people heard of AND1?</a> <a data-t="1914">It's a basketball shoe company. They allocated 5% of their profits to local charities, and significantly they,</a> <a data-t="1943">they're a shoe company so they had overseas factories. They worked to implement a supplier</a> <a data-t="1948">code of conduct to make sure that workers in overseas factories were treated well, safely,</a> <a data-t="1953">had good wages, et cetera. <p><a data-t="1958">So what happened to AND1? Well,</a> <a data-t="1964">they were pretty small in the mid-90s. They took on external investors in 1999, and it</a> <a data-t="1969">turned out that they grew quite a bit over, over that span from 95 to 2001. <p><a data-t="2012">So they got together with a friend of theirs to start a nonprofit called</a> <a data-t="2019">B-Lab, and the idea behind B-Lab was to create a new form of corporate governance. <p><a data-t="2051">I won't read this in detail, but you can see</a> <a data-t="2057">a little bit of the actual legislation behind Delaware Public Benefit Corporations, and</a> <a data-t="2062">it puts a stringent legal requirement on the directors to act in a different way, to not</a> <a data-t="2066">use the shareholder primacy regime.
