<h3>Mistakes as pedagogy</h3>

<p><a data-t="329">Next thing I'm going to say is the mistakes are the pedagogy.</a> <a data-t="334">Depending on whose numbers you trust, a professional programmer spends 20% to 60% of their time</a> <a data-t="340">debugging.</a> <a data-t="341">A novice spends 80% to 95% of their time debugging because they're a novice.</a> <a data-t="348">The same is going to be true in most areas.</a> <a data-t="351">So please don't worry about making mistakes in front of your learners when you're teaching</a> <a data-t="357">online for the first or the second or the third time because every time you make a mistake</a> <a data-t="362">and then say, oh, this is wrong, here's how I can tell, here's how I figured out how to</a> <a data-t="367">fix it, and here's how I can tell it's been fixed, they will have learned something valuable.</a></p>

<h3>Reading the room online</h3>

<p><a data-t="419">The corollary to this is it's really, really hard to teach to a room full of empty chairs.</a> <a data-t="426">All of us all the time are picking up social cues.</a> <a data-t="431">We're constantly peripherally aware of whether somebody is nodding or whether they're checking</a> <a data-t="436">their phone.</a> <a data-t="437">Did they just yawn or are they thinking because they're about to ask a question?</a> <a data-t="441">You might not notice that you're noticing, but you notice.</a> <a data-t="445">And as soon as you start teaching online, a lot of that is taken away.</a> <a data-t="450">If you take all of it away, you're going to find it very, very difficult to teach.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="458">What I suggest you do is get your students into some sort of webinar like this, but then</a> <a data-t="466">have a few of them turn on their video so that you can see a few people.</a> <a data-t="471">If, for example, you're using Zoom, which is my preferred video conferencing tool, take</a> <a data-t="477">a half a dozen names at random and ask them to turn on video, and then focus your attention</a> <a data-t="483">on those six.</a> <a data-t="485">Ask them your questions.</a> <a data-t="487">Pay attention to their faces just as you would in a real class.</a> <a data-t="491">And in your next class, pick a different half dozen.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="495">This teaching practice predates video.</a> <a data-t="498">It was quite common.</a> <a data-t="499">It still is in some places.</a> <a data-t="500">If you've got a large class, to pick a few people, bring them down front and focus your</a> <a data-t="505">teaching on them for this hour, but then focus it on a different half dozen for the next</a> <a data-t="510">hour and so on.</a> <a data-t="512">They get individual attention.</a> <a data-t="513">You get the kind of responsiveness you need to make improvisation and teaching lively.</a> <a data-t="520">And everybody else now has permission to turn off their video and reduce the load on the</a> <a data-t="524">system.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="525">If you're going to do this, please give people a way to opt out.</a> <a data-t="530">For a whole bunch of reasons, a lot of people are uncomfortable being on video online.</a> <a data-t="536">If you've ever had to deal with harassment or a stalker, you don't want images of yourself</a> <a data-t="541">appearing that might go out of your control.</a> <a data-t="543">People have many other issues.</a> <a data-t="546">If they're working at home for the first time, for example, maybe they don't want others</a> <a data-t="550">to be able to see the mess in the background, the kids playing, and so on.</a> <a data-t="555">So invite people, don't mandate it, but try to get, as I said, four, five, or six people</a> <a data-t="560">that you're directing your teaching to.</a> <a data-t="563">That will keep you engaged and you'll do a better job with the teaching.</a></p>

<h3>Directing traffic and managing audio</h3>

<p><a data-t="568">The next one was recommended by one of my colleagues, and I'm embarrassed that I didn't</a> <a data-t="573">think of it because it reveals my expert blind spot.</a> <a data-t="577">The first time you move your class online, give them a tour of the tool that you're using.</a> <a data-t="584">They probably haven't seen Zoom or whatever you've got.</a> <a data-t="588">They might not know where to click the button to mute.</a> <a data-t="591">Seems obvious once you know, it's anything but if you've just been thrown into it.</a> <a data-t="597">So taking 10 minutes at the start of your first online lecture to show people how you're</a> <a data-t="602">expecting them to use the tool, or taking an hour beforehand to take a few screenshots</a> <a data-t="609">and then share those with the class, can save a lot of grief.</a> <a data-t="614">If you're going to do this, make sure that you're logged in as a normal user, not as</a> <a data-t="620">the teacher or the conference organizer, when you're giving the tool.</a> <a data-t="625">Because if you're logged in as a super user, you will see a different set of tools that</a> <a data-t="630">they don't have, and that will confuse them.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="636">The next one up is to direct traffic.</a> <a data-t="640">You don't have to go full North Korean, that's where this picture's from.</a> <a data-t="644">But again, for a lot of people, this is their first time being in some sort of structured</a> <a data-t="649">formal setting online.</a> <a data-t="651">So always give them verbal cues.</a> <a data-t="655">Ask them to put their questions in the chat.</a> <a data-t="658">Ask them to add their answers to a homework question to the shared Google Doc.</a> <a data-t="664">Call on them by name when you want them to speak, or ask them to type something like</a> <a data-t="670">slash hand or raise hand in the chat when they want to speak.</a> <a data-t="676">Make sure that they're all getting direction about what they're supposed to do next.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="683">After a while, you can dial that down.</a> <a data-t="686">But I have found in my own teaching that I don't.</a> <a data-t="689">I constantly do it because I've constantly got people who are new or who are coming in</a> <a data-t="695">from somebody else's class where they might have evolved a different set of conventions.</a> <a data-t="701">Whatever it is, you as the tour guide telling people now we're moving on to the Renaissance</a> <a data-t="706">Arts section, everybody step this way, again, it takes away a lot of the uncertainty.</a> <a data-t="712">And right now, people have got enough uncertainty to deal with.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="718">One other point on this, mute early and mute often.</a> <a data-t="723">By default now when I'm in Zoom, after somebody finishes speaking, I don't wait for them to</a> <a data-t="729">mute.</a> <a data-t="730">I don't even mute them.</a> <a data-t="732">I instinctively now hit the mute all button.</a> <a data-t="735">I put everybody back in a muted state, even if they don't need to be, because that way</a> <a data-t="741">I never forget to.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="744">You can do a very good class audio only or audio with slides.</a> <a data-t="749">Video is helpful, but it's not essential.</a> <a data-t="753">And I can prove this by asking, what would happen if we left you with video of me talking</a> <a data-t="757">right now but turned off the sound?</a> <a data-t="761">You wouldn't learn anything.</a> <a data-t="763">You've got to make sure that everybody can be heard.</a> <a data-t="767">You've got to make sure that you're not drowned out by feedback, by background noise, or crucially</a> <a data-t="773">by somebody who constantly wants to be the next one to ask the question and constantly</a> <a data-t="778">wants to jump in first.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="782">One thing that I do to ensure that is I actually keep a written list of names beside me of</a> <a data-t="789">the people that I'm focusing my teaching on, that half dozen I referred to earlier.</a> <a data-t="793">Every time I interact with them, I just put a tick beside their name.</a> <a data-t="797">So that I can very quickly see who I have been talking to and who I haven't been talking to.</a> <a data-t="804">That way I can distribute my attention more or less fairly among those half dozen.</a> <a data-t="809">And if somebody is constantly trying to jump in, I can say, no, it's not your turn.</a> <a data-t="815">We're trying to take turns here.</a> <a data-t="817">If you are relying on people to unmute themselves and just jump in with their questions, your</a> <a data-t="823">class is going to be dominated by a couple of extroverts.</a> <a data-t="826">And everybody else will very quickly learn to not even bother to try to ask questions</a> <a data-t="831">or to contribute ideas because they can never get in fast enough.</a> <a data-t="835">Mute early, mute often, and then have people request the microphone in order to speak so</a> <a data-t="841">that you do the unmuting.</a> <a data-t="844">And as I said, call on people by name.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="846">If I ask the room, are there any questions, it will be followed with silence.</a> <a data-t="854">Sometimes that silence means nobody has any questions.</a> <a data-t="858">Sometimes that silence means people have questions, but they don't want to look foolish.</a> <a data-t="862">And sometimes that silence means they're very busy typing and the question is going to pop</a> <a data-t="867">up in 15 seconds, by which point I will probably have moved on.</a> <a data-t="873">Instead, when I want somebody to contribute, I call on them by name.</a> <a data-t="879">And if they want to pass, if they say, I don't have an answer, I don't feel like this is the</a> <a data-t="883">right time, that's fine, I'll move on to the next person.</a></p>

<h3>Don't teach alone</h3>

<p><a data-t="927">This is all a lot to manage.</a> <a data-t="929">So this brings us to the other big takeaway.</a> <a data-t="933">Don't teach alone.</a> <a data-t="934">If you can possibly avoid it, don't be the only person trying to run things.</a> <a data-t="940">Every great surgeon has a great nurse beside her.</a> <a data-t="944">Every great teacher has a co-teacher.</a> <a data-t="947">It could be a fellow instructor, it could be a TA, it could be one of your learners that</a> <a data-t="953">you've approached to say, you seem to be okay with this material, can I ask you a favor?</a></p>

<p><a data-t="991">It doesn't take a lot of practice to learn how to do that, and it does also mean that</a> <a data-t="999">if next week you're unable to make a scheduled class for whatever reason, there's somebody</a> <a data-t="1006">else who's at least partly familiar with what you're teaching and with how you're delivering it.</a></p>

<h3>Setup tips: notes, slides, and accessibility</h3>

<p><a data-t="1081">Another small note, keep your notes beside you.</a> <a data-t="1088">I've got a laptop on my desk and then I've got a large screen monitor above it.</a> <a data-t="1093">I think that's a fairly common setup these days.</a> <a data-t="1096">What that meant was that going back a while, whenever I was teaching, I was looking up</a> <a data-t="1102">and had my head tilted back, and my daughter eventually told me that as far as she was</a> <a data-t="1106">concerned, my webcam was a nose cam, and that's why I don't have a picture for this slide.</a> <a data-t="1113">If you've got your notes beside you, it's very natural to turn your head to the side,</a> <a data-t="1117">check what you're going to say next, and come back to your lecture.</a> <a data-t="1120">If you've got the slides up here, it's not something you're used to doing, and the angle</a> <a data-t="1126">can be rather unflattering.</a> <a data-t="1127">I know it's a small thing, but the small things add up.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="1133">A much larger thing, please add speaker's notes to your slides.</a> <a data-t="1140">You are about to discover that a lot of your learners have trouble seeing or hearing.</a> <a data-t="1145">They've got good compensating strategies or accommodation strategies in a live classroom</a> <a data-t="1150">because they've got a lifetime of experience doing this.</a> <a data-t="1154">They may not have the same support now that you're bailing everything online.</a> <a data-t="1161">If you put speaker's notes in your slides, it makes the slides more findable because</a> <a data-t="1167">search tools know how to index them.</a> <a data-t="1169">It makes the slides more usable because people can understand after the fact what you meant</a> <a data-t="1174">by that picture, but crucially, it makes them more accessible, and however hard you think</a> <a data-t="1181">life is suddenly being thrown online, it's a lot harder if you're partially sighted or</a> <a data-t="1188">fully deaf.</a> <a data-t="1190">Please don't make life harder for people who already have more to struggle with.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="1196">In fact, using accessibility tools helps everyone.</a> <a data-t="1200">I don't know if you can see my cursor right now, but I've used the accessibility tools</a> <a data-t="1206">on the Mac to make it as large as possible so that it's easier for people to see on video.</a> <a data-t="1213">I do that whenever I'm teaching.</a> <a data-t="1216">I use an enlarged cursor because a small cursor in a small window is really, really hard to see.</a> <a data-t="1222">I switch to high contrast mode so that people who might be sizing down the screen or might</a> <a data-t="1228">be on older devices or might just have crappy monitors have a better chance of being able</a> <a data-t="1233">to see what's going on.</a></p>

<h3>Using predictions and formative assessment</h3>

<p><a data-t="1856">Another way that you can take advantage of the online teaching is to ask people for predictions.</a> <a data-t="1862">This is particularly important when you're teaching coding.</a> <a data-t="1866">If I'm teaching a programming class, I'm probably sharing my screen so that you can see the</a> <a data-t="1871">code as I'm building it.</a> <a data-t="1874">Every time I'm about to do something, I have an opportunity to say, all right, everybody,</a> <a data-t="1879">in the chat, what's going to happen if I run it now?</a> <a data-t="1883">I've just added this line, how's it going to behave?</a></p>

<p><a data-t="1887">Pedagogically, this is forcing people to pull recent knowledge back into consciousness and</a> <a data-t="1893">use it and reinforce it.</a> <a data-t="1895">That will help them learn and remember.</a> <a data-t="1898">From a motivational point of view, it increases their engagement.</a> <a data-t="1901">They're constantly being asked to think and to contribute, and from your point of view,</a> <a data-t="1907">it gives you a very quick formative assessment.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="1911">For those who don't know the term, formative assessment is what you do during learning</a> <a data-t="1916">to find out whether or not people are learning.</a> <a data-t="1919">It's your real-time feedback loop.</a> <a data-t="1922">You cannot trust people to go, uh-huh, I got it, I got it, because a lot of people will</a> <a data-t="1927">lie.</a> <a data-t="1928">They'll say they have it when they don't, or they'll believe that they do, but they'll</a> <a data-t="1931">be wrong.</a> <a data-t="1932">And they're even more likely to mislead this way when you're teaching online.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="1938">If on the other hand, I say, all right, Paul and Ahmed, what's this going to do next?</a> <a data-t="1944">If they're right, you know that they're understanding.</a> <a data-t="1949">If they're wrong, then you know that you've got something to explain, to clarify, or to</a> <a data-t="1954">cover a second time, and you can do it right then and there before their misconception</a> <a data-t="1958">has a chance to set in concrete.</a></p>
