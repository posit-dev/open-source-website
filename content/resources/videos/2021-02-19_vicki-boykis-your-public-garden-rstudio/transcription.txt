<h3>Radio 2020 and the noise of digital life</h3>

<p><a data-t="225">So after being pinged all morning and exposed to the stressful world of 2020 during</a> <a data-t="232">COVID and the amplification of that world in my digital media, I start hearing not the individual</a> <a data-t="238">noise, but the steady stream of Radio KFKD. <p><a data-t="272">Today we're living in Radio KFKD, but over top of</a> <a data-t="281">that is the pandemic. So it's what I like to call Radio 2020. <p><a data-t="317">Being isolated is hard enough as a human. <p><a data-t="352">Why is radio 2020 so good at derailing us?</a> <a data-t="361">And why is it constantly creating noise for us? There's three reasons. <p><a data-t="421">Sometimes this engagement is good, like if we're discovering interesting blog posts, or donating</a> <a data-t="426">to creators, or watching original content. <p><a data-t="446">A second problem with this content is a lot of it is very superlative.</a> <a data-t="451">What do I mean when I say superlative? <p><a data-t="499">The third problem is that online is worse in the context of the current</a> <a data-t="505">state of the internet. <h3>Building your own digital garden</h3>

<p><a data-t="526">So we have this crisis that's occurring both in the physical world, with everything that's</a> <a data-t="532">been happening during the pandemic, and in the digital world with a crisis for our attention as well.</a> <a data-t="538">How do we fight this? <p><a data-t="555">Anne Lamott in Bird by Bird says that the best thing to do is to take it piece by piece, and the</a> <a data-t="562">title of the book comes from a book report that her brother had to write. 30 years ago, she writes,</a> <a data-t="568">my older brother, who was 10 years old at the time, was trying to get a report on birds that he'd</a> <a data-t="573">written that he had three months to write. <p><a data-t="598">The idea is that we can't change everything, but even changing a little tiny bit of our environment</a> <a data-t="603">is better than changing nothing. <p><a data-t="609">The Secret Garden is a book</a> <a data-t="614">by Frances Hogson Burnett, who lived in America and England in the 19th century. <p><a data-t="667">So right now, we're all a little</a> <a data-t="673">bit like Mary, at the start of a garden that's fallow. We're lost. We're not quite sure what to</a> <a data-t="679">do. <h3>Ways people have cultivated their digital gardens</h3>

<p><a data-t="693">So how do we fix this? How do we create something that's for our public garden</a> <a data-t="698">and makes the internet better at the same time? <h3>Smaller ways to get started</h3>

<p><a data-t="1069">But what if you're overwhelmed by</a> <a data-t="1078">the idea of getting started and don't know how?</a> <a data-t="1083">There's a lot of really small ideas that you can draw from as well, that don't necessarily involve</a> <a data-t="1088">creating a course or making videos or creating your own blog.
