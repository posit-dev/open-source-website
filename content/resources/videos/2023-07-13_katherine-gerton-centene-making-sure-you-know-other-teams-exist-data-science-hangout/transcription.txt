<h3>Risk adjustment explained</h3>

<p><a data-t="348">Awesome.</a> <a data-t="348">Well, I know you said you weren't thinking about giving an overview about risk adjustments, but while we're here and Libby asked it, so why don't we?</a> <a data-t="362">Why don't we?</a> <a data-t="362">Yeah, so when the Affordable Care Act was put into place in about 2015, one of the things that was mandated there is insurance companies can no longer deny people coverage for pre-existing conditions.</a> <a data-t="375">So it doesn't matter what conditions you have.</a> <a data-t="375">You have to get insurance.</a> <a data-t="380">You can't discriminate based on rates for those conditions that someone has.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="385">So one of the things that is in place in order to ensure that that happens is what's called risk adjustment.</a> <a data-t="390">And so throughout the year, all health insurance companies submit their claims data and their diagnosis data for their entire populations to these government-maintained servers.</a> <a data-t="402">And then you get credit for the risk.</a> <a data-t="402">So members have certain conditions that counts them as risky or not risky.</a> <a data-t="409">There's a bunch of interaction factors and a whole lot of math involved in calculating how much risk a member has.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="413">And so then at the end of the year is when the risk adjustment takes place.</a> <a data-t="420">So insurers who insure a riskier population actually get transfer dollars from the insurers who insure a less risky population.</a> <a data-t="426">So it's entirely budget neutral.</a> <a data-t="432">There's no government money involved in changing hands.</a> <a data-t="432">They facilitate that transfer between those insurers but not anything else.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="439">So if one insurer insures a bunch of like elderly people with multiple comorbidities and one insurer insures a bunch of healthy college students, the one who insures a bunch of healthy college students is going to owe money to that other insurer at the end of the year.</a> <a data-t="456">Just to say like we're covering more risk than you are.</a> <a data-t="456">And then there's the process.</a> <a data-t="463">So I have a sister team who's involved in the risk adjustment and I am in charge of the audit of the risk adjustment.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="470">So once you've submitted all of this data, the government takes a representative sample of your population and comes back to you and say prove it.</a> <a data-t="474">Prove that these members have the conditions that you say that they have.</a> <a data-t="478">So we have to go and collect medical records, talk to doctors, code all of those records, and then recalculate the risk and prove that the population distribution is as risky as we said it was throughout the year.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="497">Interesting.</a> <a data-t="497">Yeah, as Libby said in the chat, that sounds like a huge data project.</a> <a data-t="501">What are some of the projects that you work on and analytics that come after that?</a></p>

<p><a data-t="508">So there's a huge part of this that's actually competitive intelligence analytics where you know because it is budget neutral and there's transfers between different insurers, you know how risky your own population is.</a> <a data-t="520">But you don't really know a lot about the other insurer's population.</a> <a data-t="527">So there's a lot of data gathering, data aggregation around that relative risk analytics.</a> <a data-t="533">And then we also have a bunch of analytics involved with predicting which conditions are going to be easy to validate, which ones are going to be more difficult, what pieces of information might we need to gather in the aspect of conducting this audit in order to validate these conditions.</a> <a data-t="552">So that's all the like post-hoc analytics.</a> <a data-t="552">And then we also have a lot of prospective analytics around identifying members that we think may have conditions that they haven't seen their doctor for yet this year and closing those care gaps.</a></p>
