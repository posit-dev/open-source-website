description: 'To combine two data frames in R: use bind_rows to stack them, bind_cols to paste one on as extra columns, and a join when the rows have to be matched on a shared key. left_join keeps every row in the first table, inner_join keeps only the rows that appear in both, right_join and outer_join do the mirror cases, and semi_join and anti_join filter one table by what is in the other.


  What you will learn

  - When to use bind_rows, and why bind_cols needs care about row order

  - What union, intersect and setdiff do to two data frames

  - What a join key is, and what happens to rows that have no match

  - The difference between left_join, inner_join, right_join and outer_join

  - How semi_join and anti_join filter rather than combine

  - How to check a join before you rely on it
