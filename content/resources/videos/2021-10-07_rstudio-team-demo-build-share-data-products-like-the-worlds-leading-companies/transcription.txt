<h3>The hard truth of data science</h3>

<p><a data-t="259">Part of what we're covering today is the hard truth that data science is really hard</a> <a data-t="263">and it can be challenging to kind of get value from some of the work you're doing or get,</a> <a data-t="268">you know, executive buy-in or business value from what you're doing. So in many cases,</a> <a data-t="274">many data science teams actually fail to live up to what they promise or what they want to deliver</a> <a data-t="278">because there's these challenges or these kind of momentum that they have to fight against.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="284">So what we've done with RStudio team is try and solve some of these problems. And first,</a> <a data-t="288">let's kind of talk about the problems or the barriers that data science teams might face,</a> <a data-t="293">and then we'll talk about how we can work to solve those. So first off, data science teams</a> <a data-t="298">find it difficult to create insights or impact decision-making or find it difficult to create,</a> <a data-t="304">maintain, and improve their data products, their models, their APIs, their applications over time.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="311">There's a lot of different ways that this shows up in terms of it could be difficult to recruit</a> <a data-t="315">data scientists because you're using a very specific proprietary tool or you don't have</a> <a data-t="320">all your data connected. For decision-making, maybe your stakeholders just don't understand</a> <a data-t="325">the insights or there's a long, you know, duration between what if this happens or what if this</a> <a data-t="331">happens and the actual answer to that question. It's also often difficult for teams to build on</a> <a data-t="337">previous work for new use cases. They basically have to reinvent the wheel every time rather than</a> <a data-t="341">kind of building off previous work.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="341">Also, they might have analysis constraints or delays imposed</a> <a data-t="348">by some of those proprietary tools. This can lead to slow iteration, which hinders alignment between</a> <a data-t="353">the business unit and the data science team, or even irrelevant or outdated insights because things</a> <a data-t="359">have been slowed down. Additionally, this can lead to insights become obsolete or difficult to</a> <a data-t="364">reproduce. Again, the reproducibility kind of tenet of RStudio is true for both academia</a> <a data-t="372">and for business settings.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="372">And then lastly, siloed teams often lead to redundant work and there's lack</a> <a data-t="378">of collaboration with too much time just spent like maintaining the tools or the difficulty of</a> <a data-t="383">maintaining data science environments. Lastly, you know, teams can't often self-deploy. So they have</a> <a data-t="390">to like go ask IT, can you take this and run with it or hand it off to a new team? And there's this</a> <a data-t="394">disconnect between what you're actually creating and getting it into decision-making or getting it</a> <a data-t="399">into production. And this is kind of lastly pointed out by just too much manual effort. You know, you're</a> <a data-t="405">not able to rerun your analysis. You're not able to maintain the, you know, the work that you're creating.</a></p>

<h3>Serious data science: RStudio's approach</h3>

<p><a data-t="412">So part of what we try and approach this from is here at RStudio, we try and solve these problems</a> <a data-t="417">in terms of creating insights, impact and decision-making, maintain and improve over time</a> <a data-t="422">things that are difficult by focusing on our three kind of core tenets. Open source work,</a> <a data-t="428">a code-first mentality, and centralized or cloud-based kind of data science environments</a> <a data-t="433">and production.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="433">So open source basically means widely used. It eases recruiting, retention and</a> <a data-t="440">training. You have an entire massive community in R and Python creating content that you can learn</a> <a data-t="445">from, as well as libraries and different code examples that you can adapt. Code-first meaning</a> <a data-t="451">it's flexible. You can actually craft it into exactly what you want. There's not this black</a> <a data-t="456">box mentality where you just kind of plug something in and get something back. You understand the</a> <a data-t="460">process. And then centralized or cloud-based by reducing the necessary work and enhancing</a> <a data-t="466">collaboration, again, I'm working from a browser so I can access my work from anywhere and collaborate</a> <a data-t="472">with my colleagues even though we're all remote in this time.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="472">Open source also means things like</a> <a data-t="477">comprehensive. So based on community contributions, there's tens of thousands of R packages on CRAN</a> <a data-t="484">and tens of thousands of Python libraries across PyPI or other locations. Code-first also means</a> <a data-t="491">you can iterate quickly and update quickly and you can adapt the code and understand by just</a> <a data-t="496">changing small portions. And then for centralized work, your deployment provides stakeholders</a> <a data-t="502">self-service access. So you can actually say here's the data product I've created or the model</a> <a data-t="507">that we're using and have them actually interact with that as well as have it work against, say,</a> <a data-t="511">your website in production.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="511">And then lastly, open source means interoperable. So you can break down</a> <a data-t="518">some of these analytic silos. You're not stuck with a specific vendor. You can move between clouds</a> <a data-t="523">or even move between products because you own the kind of data science code that you've written</a> <a data-t="528">there in terms of you're able to adapt it. Code-first meaning reusable and fully extensible. So again,</a> <a data-t="534">you can diff it. You can put into version control and check all your changes and see what did we do</a> <a data-t="539">two years ago versus what we're doing today. And lastly, package management is something that</a> <a data-t="545">we're very keen on to support, again, reproducibility and easing the administration of open source data</a> <a data-t="550">science in your enterprise.</a></p>

<p><a data-t="550">So kind of summarizing this down, we've talked a little bit about these</a> <a data-t="556">different slides. This serious data science concept is summarized as open source, code-first,</a> <a data-t="562">centralized computation, or a cloud environment. The leading languages that we're focused on is</a> <a data-t="567">R and Python. That's what we see the most of the data science teams using today, and that's where</a> <a data-t="571">we've built a lot of our tooling around.</a></p>
